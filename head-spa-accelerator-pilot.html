What are you actually building?

A head spa is not defined by the bed alone. Before you shop for equipment or write a menu, decide what kind of experience you want to offer—and what you can reliably deliver.

01 / Understand the difference

1 of 3 sections

01 / Learn & observe

The equipment doesn't make the promise.

A client isn't paying for a piece of furniture. They are choosing an appointment with a particular purpose: time for scalp care, a relaxing experience, or a combination of both.

Those services can look different. A focused scalp service may work from an existing wash station. A dedicated head spa appointment usually needs more preparation, a deliberate treatment environment, and a planned beginning and ending. Neither format is automatically right for every business.

Model A · Focused scalp service

A defined addition to an existing visit.

A shorter, more targeted scalp-care experience built around a clear client need and the equipment already available, where professional scope permits.

  • Limited time and space
  • A clearly stated service, not a misleading full-appointment claim
  • Requires its own sanitation, consultation and scheduling decisions
Model B · Dedicated head spa appointment

A complete visit with its own rhythm.

A planned appointment where arrival, comfort, scalp-focused treatment, transitions and closing are intentionally connected.

  • More control of the environment and pacing
  • Separate setup and reset requirements
  • Time must be justified by client experience, not just equipment
Practitioner reasoning

Don't ask only, “Which bed should I buy?” Ask, “What am I promising a client, what can I provide within my professional scope, and what must happen before and after the hands-on service?”

02 / Think like a practitioner

More minutes need a reason.

AIMT's reference service formats show a useful distinction: an extended experience does not simply stretch every step. Some steps are constrained by product directions, sanitation, and the client's needs. Other portions can intentionally expand.

Example · Core versus Extended

A 60-minute reference and a 90-minute reference are not automatically interchangeable. The additional time has to make a meaningful difference to the appointment, without making rinses or product processing longer merely to fill the clock.

The 60-minute appointment that takes longer than an hour.

Before you choose a price or decide how many services you can fit in a day, count all the time this one appointment occupies. Change any of the assumptions below.

The complete time commitment

100 minutes of appointment capacity, including 40 minutes outside the hands-on service.

If your calendar blocks only 60 minutes, the schedule is missing preparation, consultation, checkout and reset. Measure these before pricing or publishing your first appointment.

This measures time, not profit. AIMT's existing Service Cost Worksheet adds your real labor, supplies and overhead after you've checked the timing.

Your first decision

A colleague says: “To offer real head spa, you have to buy a dedicated bed right away. A focused scalp service at a wash station doesn't count.” You're considering head spa but haven't verified your space or client demand.

What's the most responsible next step?

You can begin with an honest, focused offering and develop a dedicated experience later—or start with the dedicated format if your service plan, training and environment support it. The correct order is promise → feasibility → equipment, not equipment → invented promise.
