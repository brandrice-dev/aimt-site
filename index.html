Module 6 — Conditions & Disorders
Checkpoint · revision shown
Checkpoint

A client comes in with visible flaking and says she's been using a zinc-based anti-dandruff shampoo for three months but it keeps getting worse. Under the microscope you see fine white powdery flakes, minimal oil, and a matte scalp surface. What's happening — and what do you tell her?

You

I'm not really sure . . .

No worries — let's work through it. Start with what the microscopy is telling you: fine white powdery flakes, minimal oil, matte surface. Does that picture match dandruff, or something else? Once you identify what's actually going on, the conversation with the client and your next steps will follow naturally. Take a shot at it.

You

Fine white flakes with minimal oil and a matte surface reads more like a dry, over-cleansed scalp than a fungal dandruff pattern. I'd describe what I'm seeing rather than label it, suggest that the client ease off of the zinc, and focus on restoring moisture.

Solid read. You tied the matte surface and minimal oil to barrier disruption rather than Malassezia activity — and your scope framing around describing what you see is exactly right. That distinction matters when a client has been self-treating for months and is expecting a diagnosis. Clean checkpoint.
