02 · Read the Timer

Use landmarks, not pressure.

The Timer is a pacing companion. It helps you see where you are in the complete service, what you are doing now, and what transition is coming next. It is not a pass/fail stopwatch and should never override client comfort, safety, product directions or practitioner judgment.

Lets you anticipate the next transition before the current block ends, especially when equipment, water, products or positioning need to change.

05

Communication cues

Say when needed is reserved for positioning, temperature, pressure, comfort or a meaningful transition. If they ask gives you a short answer for a direct client question; it is not an instruction to narrate the service.

Explain intentionally, not continuously

The Head Spa service is relaxation-first and may remain intentionally quiet. Intake establishes the plan; the service executes it. Speak when the client needs information to stay comfortable, when a transition genuinely benefits from orientation, or when they ask you a direct question.

Before the clock

Aromatherapy and Client Positioning + Comfort are part of the protocol but occur before the treatment timer begins. The treatment clock starts with Dry Brushing + Hair Play at 60:00 in Core or 90:00 in Extended.

03 · Protocol Specification

The clock follows the protocol.

Core and Extended describe how the same service framework contracts or expands. They are not menu names, and they are not universal industry appointment lengths. In this Timer, AIMT uses two concrete reference protocols so students can learn pacing from a real service structure.

Why this matters

More time does not mean more time everywhere. The 90-minute reference earns its additional time primarily through expandable experience-building portions.

Some transitions, rinses and product requirements are constrained by what the service actually needs. They should not be stretched merely to fill time.

At the other end, a service cannot be compressed indefinitely. Once important components must be removed rather than shortened, it becomes a deliberately different protocol.

Current AIMT Timer scope: the Core (60) and Extended (90) reference protocols. A different service length needs its own deliberately designed pacing map, not a compressed or stretched version of this one — for now, this Timer runs Core and Extended.
