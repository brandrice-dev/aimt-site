## What the module is really about

Module 11 is a **pro-AI module.** It teaches competent, verified use of AI
as part of modern professional practice — not fear of it, and not a
software tutorial. Students have already experienced AIMT's own
human-led/AI-assisted philosophy firsthand through Cadence before they ever
reach this module. The lesson makes that lived experience explicit: AIMT
deliberately builds AI into how it teaches, while keeping curriculum
authority, judgment, and final accountability human. The module treats AI
as leverage a practitioner directs — never as a second, competing
authority in the room.

---

## Central practitioner payoff

Verified against `module-11.md`'s core thesis and approved outcomes. The
payoff is not "how to use an AI tool" — it is knowing **where a
practitioner's judgment must stay in charge** as AI becomes part of
ordinary practice:

- AI can accelerate drafts, research, and business thinking — the
  practitioner still owns the message, the verification, and the decision.
- Clients are already arriving having consulted AI themselves; the
  practitioner's job is to receive that calmly and return the conversation
  to what can be responsibly established in person — not to ridicule the
  client or the tool, and not to rubber-stamp the AI's answer.
- A confidence score from a scalp/hair analysis tool is information, not a
  verdict — it can shape attention, never replace professional judgment.
- Whatever the practitioner ultimately says or does with a client remains
  the practitioner's responsibility, regardless of which tool informed it.

---

## Beginner misconception or mistake corrected

Two, addressed at a conceptual level only:

- Treating "AI" as one single, uniform thing rather than several different
  categories of tool suited to different jobs.
- Assuming a client who arrives with an AI-sourced answer must either be
  corrected/dismissed outright or agreed with automatically — rather than
  met with a calm, professional process that returns to what's actually
  observable today.

## Approved learning rhythm

Module 11's density is **light-to-moderate**, deliberate and instructional
rather than interaction-heavy — a single formal ungraded interaction
(Build Your B.R.I.E.F.) plus two required checkpoints, with the module's
other teaching moments (the trust matrix, the confidence-score example,
Hear/Observe/Boundary/Next Step) taught through explanation and worked
example. Per `module-11.md`, nothing beyond the following exists or should
be implied to exist:

1. **Build Your B.R.I.E.F.** (ungraded) — one practice workspace where the
   student strengthens a weak starting prompt using the B.R.I.E.F.
   framework; no score, no persistence, no completion gate.
2. Two required checkpoints (`m11cp1` — responding to a client's AI
   result; `m11cp2` — writing a real, well-formed AI request with
   verification named).

## Claims and language that must not be reintroduced

Verified against `module-11.md`'s locked structure and approved Cadence
behavior. Video scripting must not restore or introduce:

- any claim that AI is always correct, or any claim that AI is inherently
  bad or something to fear — the module is deliberately pro-AI-used-well,
  not anti-AI;
- a confidence score, percentage, or AI-generated result presented or
  narrated as an actual diagnosis;
- any list of specific current AI products/brands presented as required
  or endorsed tools — the module teaches durable categories, not products;
- a prompting/"how to write a prompt" tutorial — B.R.I.E.F. is taught
  inside the lesson itself, not summarized as technique in the opening;
- privacy/data-handling instruction of any kind — 11.6's Need/Minimize/
  Verify framework belongs inside the lesson, not the opening;
- detailed scanner-confidence-score mechanics or how such scores are
  computed — 11.4's literacy point is conceptual, not technical;
- the old course name ("HeadSpa Mastery") anywhere in Module 11's own
  framing;
- any claim that Cadence has personal human practitioner experience;
- any suggestion that using AI expands a practitioner's professional or
  legal scope/authority;
- ridicule of a client who has consulted AI, or ridicule of AI as a tool;
- reveal of `m11cp1`/`m11cp2`'s model answers or Cadence's evaluation of a
  live response.

## Presenter emphasis

Calm, current, professionally confident — not a tech evangelist, not
alarmist, not dismissive. The instructor should speak to students as
practitioners who are already encountering AI in daily life and practice,
not as newcomers being introduced to the concept of AI for the first time.
The message: *"You already use these tools somewhere in your life. Now
let's use them well — as a practitioner, with your judgment still in
charge."*

## Video boundaries

**The opening video should:**

- establish that AI is already part of modern professional practice —
  business, marketing, communication, research, and increasingly the
  conversations clients bring into the room;
- reference that students have already experienced AIMT's own human-led/
  AI-assisted approach through Cadence, and name that as the same
  philosophy this module makes explicit;
- state AIMT's position plainly: AI is leverage, not a replacement for
  practitioner judgment;
- acknowledge, briefly, that clients increasingly arrive having already
  consulted AI about their scalp or hair;
- mention, at a conceptual glimpse level only, that modern scalp-analysis
  and practice tools exist and that the module teaches how to evaluate
  them, without teaching the mechanics;
- close on the practitioner's continued responsibility for whatever
  ultimately gets communicated to or acted on with a client, regardless of
  which tool informed it;
- move the student into the lesson with curiosity, not summary.

**The opening video should not:**

- become a prompting tutorial or a "how to write a good AI request"
  lesson;
- teach privacy/data-handling practices in any detail;
- teach or dramatize scanner-confidence-score mechanics;
- list, name, rank, or endorse specific AI products or brands;
- become an anti-AI warning or cautionary lesson;
- reveal `m11cp1` or `m11cp2`'s model answers or Cadence's evaluation of a
  live response;
- read or list the AIMT AI Practice Toolkit's interior fields on screen;
- describe or characterize Module 12's actual final-exam content beyond
  naming course completion and certification as what comes next — Module
  12's Final Exam redesign has not been authorized or begun.
