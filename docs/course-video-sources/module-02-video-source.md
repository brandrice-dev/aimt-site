## What the module is really about

Module 2 teaches the arrival sequence as a professional framework rather
than a fixed ritual: the standards (preparation, privacy, communication,
consent, a calm transition) must be consistent for every client, but the
specific rituals (tea, scent, exact first-touch method) have to adapt to
that client's preferences, allergies, and comfort. Its throughline is
consent and client autonomy — nothing about the arrival should ever be
presented as something that happens *to* the client rather than *with*
them.

## Approved outcomes

- Explain why arrival and transition influence comfort, trust, and the
  client's ability to settle into the service.
- Review intake information and confirm relevant changes directly with the
  client.
- Give clear, respectful preparation instructions without requiring
  unnecessary undressing.
- Offer hospitality (tea, scent) as optional, not physiological treatment.
- Obtain explicit consent before introducing touch.
- Guide a stressed or late client without shaming or rushing them.

## Central practitioner payoff

Gives the student a repeatable five-moment sequence (2.1–2.5) they can run
with any client while still adapting it in real time — so "consistency"
stops meaning "identical every time" and starts meaning "the same standard
of care every time."

## Beginner misconception or mistake corrected

That a hospitality ritual (tea, scent, a specific first-touch method) is a
therapeutic intervention or a mandatory step, and that consent obtained once
(an intake form, a prior visit, closed eyes) covers everything that follows.
The module corrects both: rituals are optional and adaptable; touch requires
its own explicit permission every time.

## Insider knowledge

- Rushing a client because the *practitioner's* schedule is behind
  transfers that pressure onto the client and is the single most damaging
  thing that can happen in the first five minutes — worse than any missing
  amenity.
- A declined ritual (no tea, no scent) is not a problem to solve — it's a
  valid client choice that should be supported without any visible
  friction.
- Explaining what's about to happen ("I'll rest a hand on your shoulder —
  is that okay?") removes uncertainty without needing to manufacture a
  feeling; that's the actual mechanism, not some claimed nervous-system
  effect.
- The professional test after any service is not "did they enjoy the
  ritual" but "did this client receive the same standard of privacy,
  choice, and consent as the client before them."

## Approved learning rhythm

Higher interaction density than the Welcome Module or Module 1: an
accessible five-step accordion (2.1–2.5) for content navigation, an ungraded
"What breaks the moment?" judgment check, an ungraded aromatherapy-script
writing exercise with Cadence feedback, a static "Same service. Different
beginning." comparison, and one required checkpoint (`m2cp1`) at the end.
This is intentionally busier than Module 1 — the content itself is about a
live, adaptive interaction, so the module's own rhythm should feel more
active and applied rather than read-and-answer.

## Relationship to adjacent modules

Builds directly on Module 1's professional-boundary foundation by applying
it to the client's very first moments — privacy, consent, and scope-aware
preparation instructions all depend on the boundary Module 1 established.
Its approved completion copy hands off explicitly: "Module 3 moves beneath
the experience into the science of the hair and scalp. The goal is to
understand what you are observing so later service decisions have a clear
professional reason behind them."

## Claims and language that must not be reintroduced

- Any claim that tea, scent, or touch lowers nervous-system arousal,
  creates "subconscious trust," or guarantees a transformed experience or
  rebooking.
- Instructing the practitioner to place a hand on the client's shoulder
  before consent, or before the client can decline.
- Universal instructions to remove a bra, top layers, or jewelry as though
  required for every service.
- "In this order, every time" / "nothing in this module is optional"
  framing — replaced with the standards-consistent/rituals-adapt
  distinction.
- The old course names; Cadence claiming personal industry experience.

## Presenter emphasis

Warm but precise — this module is about hospitality without ever letting
hospitality slide into an unverified claim. The instructor should frame
consent and adaptability as the *professional* choice, not a soft or
optional add-on.

## Video boundaries

Preview the five-moment framework and the consent-before-touch principle.
Do not narrate the full accordion content, the four "What breaks the
moment?" options, or the checkpoint scenario in detail — the lesson covers
those. Screen-interface footage is approved for capture given completed
manual QA, but a live Cadence grading exchange or script-builder response
should be treated as unverified for exact phrasing (see Status).

## Production flags

- Live-model grading accuracy for `m2cp1` and the script-builder evaluator
  is unverified — avoid presenting a live AI response as a finished,
  guaranteed example of tone.
- Screen-reader, physical-keyboard, reduced-motion, and touch-device
  behavior are unaudited; avoid implying full accessibility verification
  in any video claim.
- No image or B-roll assets currently exist for this module.

## Suggested duration

**120–150 seconds.** The five-moment framework and the consent principle
both need room, but the module's content is concrete and example-driven
rather than dense, so it doesn't need Module 3's longer runway.
