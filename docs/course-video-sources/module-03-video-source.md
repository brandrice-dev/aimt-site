## What the module is really about

Module 3 is the course's first technical-science module: it turns anatomy
from vocabulary into a decision tool. Its central move is teaching a
repeatable reasoning sequence — structure → visible clue → service
implication → limit — so that a term like "sebaceous gland" or a concept
like the hair-growth cycle isn't memorized in isolation but is immediately
tied to a real service decision (which cleanser, how much pressure, when to
refer).

## Approved outcomes

- Explain the scalp as a specialized skin environment, not just a surface.
- Identify the five anatomical scalp layers without implying a cosmetic
  service selectively treats the deeper ones.
- Distinguish the visible hair shaft from the living hair follicle.
- Explain the hair-growth cycle (anagen/catagen/telogen/exogen) in
  practical client-facing language.
- Use the delay between a triggering event and visible shedding to reason
  more accurately about a client's history.
- Explain the scalp barrier honestly enough to avoid reflexive
  over-cleansing or over-exfoliating.
- Explain scalp massage as a controlled sensory/manual technique, not a
  proven hair-regrowth treatment.

## Central practitioner payoff

Gives the student a fast, defensible reasoning chain for the two moments
that come up constantly in real services — "why did this shedding start
now?" and "why is this scalp reacting this way?" — instead of either
guessing at a cause or reflexively reaching for a stronger product.

## Beginner misconception or mistake corrected

That a visible clue (shedding, flaking, oiliness) has one obvious cause
discoverable by looking harder, and that a recent event explains a recent
symptom. The module's signature correction is timing: a major physiological
event (illness, fever, childbirth) can take **two to three months** to show
up as visible shedding, so the beginner's instinct to look only at what
happened "this week" is actively wrong.

## Insider knowledge

- **The delay is data.** Shedding often reflects an event from months
  earlier, not days — this reframes what "history" a practitioner should
  actually ask about.
- **One symptom is not one diagnosis.** Flaking, oil, tightness, redness,
  and product history have to be read together, not treated as
  interchangeable evidence for the same conclusion.
- **The image is not the whole anatomy.** The course's own cross-section
  illustration is explicitly a simplified follicle diagram, not a complete
  five-layer scalp map — the two shouldn't be confused even by the course's
  own visuals.
- **Massage doesn't need a growth promise to be valuable.** Its real,
  honest value is technique, comfort, rhythm, and consistency — dressing it
  up as a treatment is both inaccurate and unnecessary.
- Before reaching for a stronger cleanser, the approved decision rule is
  three questions: what does the scalp feel like, what's been used on it,
  and what else is visible.

## Approved learning rhythm

**Moderate interaction density** — more active than a textbook chapter, less
dense than Module 2. Structurally distinct from Modules 0–2: it has a
signature visual explorer ("Anatomy to Action," four selectable
structures), a predict-then-reveal discovery moment ("The delay tells the
story"), and — uniquely in the course so far — a **midpoint checkpoint**
(`cp1`, placed immediately after the timing discovery) in addition to the
final checkpoint (`cp2`). The student is expected to predict the timing
answer before being told, and to answer both checkpoints before receiving
corrective feedback — independent reasoning is built into the structure,
not just the content.

## Claims and language that must not be reintroduced

- "The scalp is skin. Treat it like it." (replaced by the approved
  headline above).
- "The visible hair is not the patient. The follicle is." — too absolute
  and medically framed.
- Any claim that massage delivers nutrients to follicles, creates a
  "healthier growth environment," reduces traction-related thinning, or
  causes hair growth.
- Presenting delayed diffuse shedding or postpartum shedding as a confirmed
  diagnosis rather than a "compatible pattern."
- The old, medically-impossible checkpoint timeline (shedding described as
  starting *before* the illness that supposedly caused it).
- Treating a normal daily-shedding count as a diagnostic cutoff.
- Cadence claiming personal experience ("I didn't love studying anatomy
  when I was in school").
- The old course names.

## Presenter emphasis

Should feel like the moment the course "turns technical" — confident and
precise rather than dry or textbook-like. The instructor should lead with
insight (the delay, the layered reasoning) rather than disclaimers, saving
scope caveats for where the content specifically requires them (per the
approved Cadence-behavior pattern: structure → clue → implication → limit).

## Video boundaries

Preview the "surface clues connect to structures and timing" idea and the
delay discovery, without giving away the timing interaction's actual answer
or narrating the full hair-growth-cycle card set. Do not walk through either
checkpoint's exact scenario. Screen-interface footage is approved for
capture given completed manual QA, but any depicted Cadence response should
be flagged as unverified against the live model, and any spoken claim about
shedding timing, barrier function, or massage should be treated as pending
