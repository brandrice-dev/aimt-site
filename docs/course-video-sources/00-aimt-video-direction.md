# AIMT Video Direction — Visual & Production Reference

**Purpose:** The fixed brand/visual identity every module-opening video must
share, so the collection reads as one production even though each module's
concept, script, and shot list differ. This file covers *what AIMT looks and
sounds like*. For the per-video writing process, required 12-section output
format, and script-craft rules, see the already-approved
[`00-aimt-module-video-master-instructions.md`](00-aimt-module-video-master-instructions.md)
in this folder — do not duplicate that document's process guidance here.

**Authority:** `CLAUDE.md` (design-system rule), `docs/AIMT-Art-Direction-Brief.md`,
`docs/AIMT-AUDIT-RULES.md`, and each module's own approved `module-XX.md`
specification for image-authenticity status (currently
`docs/course-audit/modules/module-04.md` and
`docs/course-audit/modules/module-05.md` — their pre-audit `module-XX-assets.md`
intake files are not current authority once a module audit is approved).
Where a specific brand token (an exact hex value, a font family beyond
what's named below, a camera package, a fixed production standard) is not
documented in one of those files, this document describes the direction
qualitatively rather than inventing one.

---

## Visual character

Elevated, clinical-editorial, quiet — per `CLAUDE.md`'s standing description
of the whole site's aesthetic ("dark, quiet, institutional") and the Art
Direction Brief's one-sentence brief: *"clinical calm, not spa fluff;
editorial, not stocky."* If a shot would look at home in a generic day-spa
ad, it does not belong in an AIMT video. Favor one decisive, precise moment
(a hand placement, a tool detail, a composed room) over a wide "spa vibes"
establishing shot.

## Palette

The documented site palette is a near-black warm background (`#1a1814`) with
a muted taupe/greige accent (`#a3968d`), and one reserved deep oxblood
(`#38141C`) used *only* for the certificate/crest seal — never as a
general-purpose accent (see the AUDIT-RULES "STANDING COLOR RULE"). In
qualitative video-grading language, that maps to:

- **Charcoal** — the near-black warm background tone; the base the whole
  video should sit on, in both wardrobe and set/background choices.
- **Warm limestone** — cream/warm-neutral surfaces and wardrobe tones;
  the page's light-value tones.
- **Mushroom** — the documented taupe/greige accent; useful for props,
  set dressing, and secondary tonal interest.
- **Restrained ochre** — not a documented site or CSS token. If a warm
  accent beyond mushroom is genuinely useful in a specific shot (a prop
  color, a practical light), keep it rare and desaturated, and do not treat
  it as an established AIMT brand color or apply it to typography, lower
  thirds, or any recurring graphic element. The only color the brand
  reserves as a true accent is the certificate seal's oxblood, and that
  stays exclusive to the crest/certificate — it must not appear as a video
  accent color.

Avoid saturated blues, greens, or pinks entirely — the Art Direction Brief
calls these out explicitly as looking like "a stranger" against AIMT's warm
palette. Apply one consistent grade across every video: slightly lifted
blacks, warm midtones, low-to-medium contrast.

## Typography and text-callout direction

The course app's documented type character is serif-led and institutional
(`CLAUDE.md`: "Cormorant Garamond / dark, quiet, institutional aesthetic").
For on-screen text and title cards, favor a quiet serif display face for
titles/eyebrows paired with a clean, restrained sans for supporting
callouts — avoid anything decorative, trendy, or condensed-bold "ad" type.
Do not invent a specific alternate font family beyond what's documented; if
a video editor needs an exact font file, that is a production decision for
the owner, not something to specify here.

Callouts should be single strong phrases, short decision rules, or brief
sequences — never full sentences functioning as subtitles, and never more
than one major thought on screen at once (see the master instructions'
"On-screen text" section for the full rule).

## Instructor-to-camera styling

Understated and professional, not commercial-beauty or influencer-styled.
Wardrobe in charcoal, limestone, or mushroom tones — consistent with the
palette above, not a separate "TV" wardrobe. Framing should be controlled
and editorial (the Art Direction Brief's "controlled light, negative space,
intention"), not a warm lifestyle vlog setup. Per the master instructions,
Cadence is never the on-camera presenter — she may be referenced as the
course guide, but the human instructor (live or voice-over) carries the
opening video.

## Lighting

Soft, controlled, warm-neutral in temperature — consistent with the site's
warm near-black background, not a cool clinical-white overhead. Avoid harsh
ring-light beauty lighting and avoid fluorescent salon overheads. Deep,
intentional shadow is acceptable and consistent with the brand; flat,
shadowless lighting is not.

## Service B-roll

One decisive moment of craft per the Art Direction Brief's shot list: hands
on a scalp, a rinse, a composed treatment-room moment — precision over a
wide "spa vibes" room shot. Subject weighted to one side of frame with
genuine negative space elsewhere, consistent with the brief's hero-image
composition guidance, rather than centered stock-photo framing.

## Technical close-ups

Macro/detail shots of tools, product, hand technique, and (where a module's
approved content covers it) scalp-surface detail. These should read as
precise documentation, not dramatic medical-photography style.

## Diagrams and animation

Reuse or extend existing approved course diagrams rather than inventing new
visual systems. The one existing production diagram asset as of this
writing is Module 3's scalp cross-section
(`assets/images/course/module-03/aimt-scalp-cross-section.png` and its
`.webp` derivative) — an approved, captioned illustration, explicitly *not*
a complete five-layer scalp diagram (see that module's video-source file).
Any new diagram or animated technical graphic must be labeled as **New
diagram needed** and is subject to the same accuracy-review discipline as
the course copy itself before being treated as final. Restrained kinetic
typography is an acceptable transition device; decorative animation for its
own sake is not.

## Course-interface footage

Screen recordings of `headspa-mastery.html` may only be captured once a
module is both implemented and has passed manual visual QA (the master
instructions' "Approved for video production" status). For a module still
at "Approved for video scripting," course-interface footage, interaction
demonstrations, and checkpoint footage must wait — script, storyboard,
presenter footage, and B-roll planning may proceed without it.

## Music and audio character

Restrained, modern, premium — per the master instructions. No loud
cinematic swells, no generic spa clichés, no singing bowls by default, no
distracting sound design. Let some moments (a checkpoint reveal, a key
professional distinction) sit in near-silence rather than being scored.

treat `docs/course-audit/modules/module-04.md`'s "Required corrections"
§14 and its "Final replacement copy" microscopy captions as the current
authority for Module 4 image status, not the stale pre-audit inventory
file):

- **Existing asset — approved** — in production use with a completed
  module audit and manual approval behind it. This currently includes
  Module 3's cross-section image/derivative; Module 4's five
  `examination-areas/` photos (real photography of assessment-point
  positioning, used as procedural guides in the five-point-scan stepper);
  and all five of Module 5's approved teaching photographs (real
  stock photography — not generated — under
  `assets/images/course/module-05/regional-comparison/` and
  `assets/images/course/module-05/service-adaptation/`; see
  `module-05-video-source.md` for placement and captions).
- **Existing asset — approved, illustrative/generated** — approved for
  production use but explicitly generated/illustration-style rather than
  authenticated clinical photography, and always captioned as such.
  Currently Module 4's five `microscopy/` images (renamed in production
  to `microscopy-baseline-appearing`, `microscopy-oil-dominant`,
  `microscopy-fine-scale`, `microscopy-visible-color-change`,
  `microscopy-surface-residue`), each carrying the approved "Illustrative
  magnified example — not a clinical diagnosis" framing.
- **Existing asset — unverified/unapproved** — present in the repository
  but not yet cleared by an approved module audit. None currently exist
  in this category; update this list as new modules extract assets ahead
  of their own audit.
- **New footage/diagram needed** — does not exist yet.

## Illustrative versus authenticated clinical imagery

Do not present an illustrative, generated, or stock-photography image as
authenticated clinical photography in any video, per the master
instructions. Concretely: Module 3's cross-section is an approved
simplified illustration, always captioned as such, never a claim of real
clinical photography. Module 4's five microscopy images are approved for
production use but are generated microscopy-style illustrations, always
captioned "Illustrative magnified example — not a clinical diagnosis" —
never settled, accurate clinical reference material. Module 4's five
examination-area photos are real photography of assessment-point
positioning (not scalp-condition evidence) and do not carry a clinical
claim either way. Module 5's five teaching photographs are real stock
photography approved for illustrative service-planning use only — always
carry the module's approved caution ("Illustrative service-planning
example. Appearance alone does not establish cause or diagnosis.") and
never treat the consultation image's background prop graphic as AIMT
documentation or an approved framework.

## Continuity elements (repeat across every video)

AIMT identity/wordmark treatment, the type system above, the restrained
charcoal/limestone/mushroom color system, the audio character described
above, a consistent title-card structure, a consistent lower-third system
for the instructor, a consistent caption treatment, consistent overall image
quality/grade, and a consistent sense of technical credibility. These should
make the collection feel like one production even across very different
module topics.

## Creative elements that should vary between modules

Opening device, visual rhythm, shot composition, metaphor, the amount of
direct-to-camera footage versus voice-over, diagram use, B-roll density,
pacing, and the final transition line. Per the master instructions: do not
open every module the same way, and do not close every module with "Let's
begin."

## Visual and language patterns to avoid

- Generic stock spa footage; unrelated candles, plants, or towels used as
  decoration with no instructional relationship.
- Excessive slow motion; a visual effect for every sentence.
- Any visual or line implying medical diagnosis, licensure, accreditation,
  guaranteed business results, or unsupported hair-growth outcomes — these
  are hard claims boundaries, not style preferences (see
  `docs/AIMT-Copy-Claims-Audit.md` and each module's approved "Claims and
  language that must not be reintroduced").
- Generated or illustrative imagery presented as authenticated clinical
  photography.
- Dramatic pathology imagery used merely for attention.
- Cliché opening lines flagged in the master instructions ("In this
  exciting module…", "Unlock the secrets…", "Transform your career…").
- Any oxblood/seal-red used as a video accent color outside the
  certificate/crest itself.
- Cadence appearing as, or being scripted as, a human on-camera presenter.
