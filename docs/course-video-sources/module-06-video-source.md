## What the module is really about

Module 6 is about interpretation under uncertainty. Module 4 taught the
student to gather evidence without overinterpreting it; Module 5 taught the
student to turn evidence into a service decision. Module 6 teaches the
student to distinguish a typical dry-scalp presentation from a
dandruff-spectrum presentation, to recognize that appearance is suggestive
and not conclusive, to use multiple observations together with client
history rather than any single visual cue, and to decide whether the
responsible next step is to proceed, modify, pause, or refer — communicating
that decision without diagnosing.

## Approved outcomes

Prioritized from `module-06.md`, "Approved outcomes," for an opening video:

- Distinguish a dry-scalp presentation from a dandruff-spectrum presentation
  using multiple observable cues together (flake color, size, oiliness,
  redness, distribution) — not one cue in isolation — and recognize when a
  presentation is ambiguous or mixed rather than forcing it into a category.
- Understand the dandruff-to-seborrheic-dermatitis relationship as one
  continuum rather than two unrelated conditions, without treating
  appearance as a confirmed diagnosis.
- Explain, without diagnosing, why matching an anti-dandruff product to a
  dry-scalp presentation can worsen it, and redirect a client caught in that
  cycle.
- Recognize the referral-relevant features that mean a presentation is
  outside cosmetic-service scope.
- Choose a within-scope, responsible cosmetic service or product-category
  direction for a described presentation.
- Communicate an observation and a service decision to a client without
  diagnosing and without contradicting or embarrassing them.

## Central practitioner payoff

The payoff is not learning condition names. It is less trial and error when
a client presents with flaking, oil, irritation, or a history of treating
the wrong problem. The student gains a more disciplined way to decide what
they are actually seeing, what remains uncertain, what changes the service,
when product escalation is the wrong move, and when referral becomes the
professional decision — foregrounding a practical reduction in
misidentification and overcorrection, not an encyclopedia of scalp
disorders.

## Beginner misconception or mistake corrected

Flakes are not automatically dandruff, and a more dramatic-looking scalp
does not automatically call for a stronger product or service. This shows
up as a common, avoidable pattern (not "most clients," per `module-06.md`'s
correction of that overgeneralization): a client sees flakes, assumes
dandruff, escalates to an anti-dandruff treatment, the underlying dry/
reactive presentation worsens, and the escalation continues rather than
resolving.

## Insider knowledge

Verified against `module-06.md`, "Practitioner insider value":

- Flake color, size, oiliness, redness, and distribution matter together —
  no single feature is conclusive on its own.
- Real presentations are often mixed or ambiguous rather than textbook-clean.
- Product and wash history can matter more than a single visual cue.
- The combination of oil, redness, spread, symptoms, and history — not any
  one feature — is what actually changes the service decision.
- Strong product escalation can perpetuate and worsen a misidentified
  dry-scalp pattern.
- When the cause is unclear, simplifying the routine and choosing a gentler
  service direction is often more responsible than escalating.
- Spread beyond the scalp with active irritation, broken/weeping/crusted
  skin, severe symptoms, rapid worsening, or genuine uncertainty all raise
  referral concern.
- Good client language can correct a client's self-diagnosis without
  contradicting or embarrassing them.

## Approved learning rhythm

Module 6 is **interpretation-led** — distinct from Module 4's
observation-led rhythm and Module 5's decision-led rhythm. Its final
approved learning rhythm includes four ungraded interactions, each with a
distinct instructional job:

1. **Dry-scalp vs. dandruff comparison** (`.vs-card`) — distinguish.
2. **"Follow the cycle"** — sequence, then apply (a progressive
   causal-sequence interaction ending in an applied "Where do you break the
   cycle?" decision).
3. **Malassezia/dandruff spectrum slider** — observe a continuum
   experientially.
4. **"Sort three presentations"** — decide/apply (the signature interaction:
   proceed as usual / proceed with modification / pause and refer).

`m6cp1` sits after the identification and product-misuse material (Sections
6.2–6.4); `m6cp2` sits at the end, after the full spectrum-and-referral
content. Module 6 differs from Module 5 in what it asks: Module 5 asks how
to adapt the service from supported observations; Module 6 asks whether the
practitioner's interpretation of what they're seeing still supports
proceeding, modifying, or referring.

## Claims and language that must not be reintroduced

Video scripting must not restore:

- diagnostic certainty from appearance;
- "this is seborrheic dermatitis" or any other named condition stated as
  confirmed fact;
- a simplistic, single-cause Malassezia/oil-driven framing (the corrected
  framing is multifactorial);
- the removed "10% per 1.8°F" temperature/sebum claim, or any other fixed
  numeric heat/sebum relationship;
- strong, established-mechanism diet-causation claims (the evidence is
  weak and individual, per the audit);
- "most clients" overgeneralization in the wrong-product-cycle narrative
  (corrected to "a common, avoidable pattern");
- the phrase "without requiring medical oversight";
- a prescription-strength (2%) ketoconazole recommendation — only
  1%-strength OTC categories are within scope;
- language implying a practitioner can diagnose a condition and then
  prescribe or direct its treatment;
- imagery described as clinical or microscopy when it is illustrative;
- old course-name language ("HeadSpa Mastery");
- any Cadence or instructor claim not supported by `module-06.md` (Cadence
  never claims personal practitioner experience).

Preserve the distinction that naming an OTC product category (1%-strength
ketoconazole, zinc pyrithione, 1% selenium sulfide) is product-category
literacy, not diagnosis or prescription. Do not make ingredient/product
discussion the hero of the opening video.

## Presenter emphasis

Controlled confidence: observant, investigative, calm, precise, with
slight tension around the idea that two similar-looking presentations can
require different responses. The student should feel "this is where
looking becomes interpretation." The presenter should not sound afraid to
make a decision — the point is disciplined judgment, not uncertainty for
its own sake. Referral should read as one legitimate professional decision
among proceed/modify/pause/refer, not the emotional climax of the video.

## Video boundaries

**The opening video should:**

- create curiosity around look-alike presentations;
- introduce the idea that flakes alone do not tell the whole story;
- preview the wrong-product-cycle problem;
- establish that trained judgment depends on multiple observations plus
  client history;
- preview that the student will learn when to proceed, modify, or refer.

**The opening video should not:**

- teach every dry-scalp/dandruff comparison bullet;
- reveal all three "Sort three presentations" answers;
- walk through the complete "Follow the cycle" solution;
- show model checkpoint answers;
- teach the entire referral list;
- turn into a pharmacology/product lecture;
- diagnose a staged client;
- imply the Section 6.3 illustration proves a condition.

Interface footage may now be captured, but interaction solutions must
remain protected so the opening video does not spoil the lesson's own
reasoning work.

## Suggested duration

**Approximately 1 minute 45 seconds to 2 minutes 15 seconds**, within the
AIMT master range of 90 seconds–2:30. Confirmed against precedent: Module 5
targeted 120–150 seconds for a decision-model concept with five supporting
teaching images. Module 6 carries comparably dense conceptual load (one
strong distinction, one practitioner decision framework: proceed / modify /
pause / refer) but only one confirmed existing visual asset, so a duration
at the shorter end of Module 5's range — rather than matching or exceeding
it — is appropriate. The video should establish one strong distinction and
the decision framework without teaching the full lesson.
