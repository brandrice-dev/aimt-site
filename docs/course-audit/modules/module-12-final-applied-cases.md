# CASE-01 — Late Arrival, New Information, Mixed Presentation

**Modules:** 2, 4, 5  
**Evidence:** D3 secondary  
**Primary competencies:** Arrival leadership; intake confirmation; regional observation; service adaptation

## Student-facing scenario

Your client arrives eight minutes late and visibly stressed. During verbal intake confirmation, she tells you she recently reacted to a scented product. That sensitivity was not listed on the intake form she submitted earlier.

During the scalp assessment you observe:

- visible residue and shine through the crown;
- fine loose scale at the temples;
- no reported tenderness, burning, or pain.

## Part A — Select all actions that belong in your response

Select **all** that apply.

A. Keep the arrival moving by shortening the intake conversation because she is already late  
B. Confirm the newly reported sensitivity and adjust the scent portion of the service accordingly  
C. Offer fragrance-free service as a valid option rather than steering her toward another scent  
D. Describe the crown and temple findings separately instead of assigning one whole-scalp type  
E. Use the crown finding to set the cleansing intensity for the whole scalp so the plan stays consistent  
F. Adapt the service by region, keeping stable areas from receiving unnecessary corrective treatment  
G. Treat the signed intake as controlling because it was completed before arrival  
H. Keep the practitioner’s communication calm and avoid transferring schedule pressure to the client

**Correct selections:** B, C, D, F, H

## Part B — Short response

> In 3–5 sentences, describe how you would adapt the service across the crown, temples, and stable areas without diagnosing the client.

## Internal rubric — 8 points

- 2 — crown receives a targeted response rather than controlling the whole scalp  
- 2 — temples receive a gentler/appropriate response based on current findings  
- 2 — stable areas are preserved rather than “corrected” unnecessarily  
- 2 — language remains descriptive/non-diagnostic

**Source:** Module 2 intake/arrival/choice; Module 4 regional observation; Module 5 regional service levers.

# CASE-02 — The Finding the Client Wants You to Ignore

**Modules:** 1, 4  
**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D1, D2, D4  
**Primary competencies:** Stop/refer; client communication; device hygiene

## Student-facing scenario

Midway through the scalp-camera assessment, you observe an area near the back of the scalp with visible fluid and mild crusting.

The client says:

> “It doesn’t hurt. I have a meeting after this, so please don’t make this into a big thing. Can we just keep going?”

Assume the camera’s contact surface touched the area before you realized the skin was compromised.

## Part A

What happens to the camera assessment?

A. Continue, but avoid direct pressure on the area  
B. Take one additional image so you have enough information to explain the concern  
C. Stop camera contact with the affected area  
D. Continue the scan elsewhere and return to the area only if the client reports discomfort

**Correct:** C

## Part B

What is the strongest service decision?

A. Continue the full service because the client is comfortable  
B. Modify the service around the area and avoid direct contact, but otherwise proceed normally  
C. Do not perform cosmetic scalp treatment over the concerning finding and explain that it should be medically evaluated before you work over that area  
D. Apply only gentle cleansing to the area because there is no pain

**Correct:** C

## Part C

What happens to the camera before it can be used again?

A. Wipe away anything visible and return it to service  
B. Remove it from service and follow the manufacturer-directed cleaning/disinfection process before reuse  
C. Continue using it on this client only, then disinfect it at closing  
D. Replace any disposable cover and return the device to the clean zone

**Correct:** B

## Part D — Short response

> What would you say to the client in this moment? Keep the explanation calm, non-diagnostic, and clear about why the service decision is changing.

## Internal rubric — 6 points

- 2 — acknowledges the client without being alarmist  
- 2 — does not name/confirm a diagnosis  
- 2 — clearly explains that the visible finding changes what can responsibly be done and supports medical evaluation

**Critical flags:**  
Type A unsafe reasoning if the student explicitly states they would continue treatment over the finding because the client is comfortable/insists, or knowingly reuse the contacted device without required reprocessing.

**Source:** Module 1 scope/referral; Module 4 do-not-proceed findings and device reprocessing.

# CASE-03 — The Escalating Home Routine

**Modules:** 5, 6  
**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D2  
**Primary competencies:** Wrong-product cycle; reassessment; service redirection

## Student-facing scenario

A returning client tells you she has been using increasingly strong anti-dandruff products for about two months because her flaking keeps getting worse.

Today you observe:

- fine, powdery white scale;
- a matte surface;
- minimal visible oil;
- no redness;
- no spread beyond the scalp margin.

She says:

> “Can you use the strongest exfoliation you have today? I really want to get all of this off.”

## Part A

Which interpretation is strongest?

A. Her routine is probably not strong enough because the flaking is still present  
B. The presentation may be more compatible with a dry-scalp pattern, so the current routine should be reassessed before anything is intensified  
C. Any visible flaking should be treated as dandruff until it responds to an OTC anti-dandruff product  
D. The presentation automatically requires medical referral because the products have not worked

**Correct:** B

## Part B

What is the strongest service decision?

A. Use strong exfoliation only where the scale is most visible  
B. Use the requested intensity because the skin is intact and the client understands what she wants  
C. Redirect to a gentler approach that avoids further unnecessary stripping/stimulation and explain why the current presentation does not support the requested intensity  
D. Skip all cleansing and exfoliation regardless of what the rest of the assessment shows

**Correct:** C

## Part C — Short response

> Explain to the client why “stronger” is not automatically the better next step here. Do not diagnose her.

## Internal rubric — 8 points

- 2 — recognizes current home routine may be mismatched to the presentation  
- 2 — does not equate visible scale with automatic exfoliation  
- 2 — redirects intensity rather than simply complying  
- 2 — explanation remains non-diagnostic and professional

**Critical flag:** Explicitly proceeding with clearly inappropriate stronger intensity solely because the client insists contributes Type A D2 evidence.

**Source:** Module 5 decision priority + scale/exfoliation rule; Module 6 wrong-product cycle.

# CASE-04 — Reset Under Real Pressure

**Modules:** 7, 10  
**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D4  
**Primary competencies:** Required process time; parallel workflow; readiness under pressure

## Student-facing scenario

Your next client arrives ten minutes early.

The room is close to ready, but:

- one hard service surface still has **three minutes remaining** in its label-directed disinfectant contact time;
- clean linens are not yet staged;
- the sensory setup is incomplete;
- an already-clean alternative surface/tool setup is available in the room.

The client can see that you are finishing the reset.

## Part A

Which action should happen regarding the surface still completing contact time?

A. Dry it now because most of the required time has already passed  
B. Preserve the full remaining contact time  
C. Cover it with a clean barrier and begin the service  
D. Rinse it with water to remove the disinfectant and finish the setup

**Correct:** B

## Part B — Select all that apply

While that time is running, which actions may be appropriate?

A. Stage the clean linens  
B. Complete other independent reset tasks  
C. Use an already-ready alternative if the service can proceed without disturbing the incomplete process  
D. Shorten the contact time if the client has already been waiting  
E. Calmly delay the service start if the required system is not ready  
F. Use an unprocessed backup item so the client does not wait

**Correct selections:** A, B, C, E

## Part C

What is the best communication choice if the room still is not ready when the appointment should begin?

A. Say nothing and move quickly so the client does not notice the delay  
B. Tell the client sanitation is taking longer than expected and ask her to waive the remaining time  
C. Calmly let the client know you need a few more minutes to finish preparing the room correctly  
D. Bring the client in and finish the reset around her

**Correct:** C

**Critical flag:** Explicit willingness to shorten required process time or use an unprocessed item is Type A D4 evidence.

**Source:** Module 7 prep sequencing; Module 10 Reset Under Pressure.

# CASE-05 — Two Different Problems Mid-Service

**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D2

## Student-facing scenario

You are in the exfoliation portion of an Extended-format service.

The client says:

> “My neck feels strained, and I’m getting a little cold.”

You make an adjustment. Several minutes later, she says:

> “The exfoliation feels like a lot today.”

There are no emergency symptoms and no new stop-and-refer finding.

## Part A — Sequence

Put the first comfort response in the strongest order:

1. Confirm comfort before resuming  
2. Stop the current action  
3. Communicate what you are adjusting  
4. Adjust positioning and/or temperature

**Correct order:** 2 → 4 → 3 → 1

## Part B

How should the exfoliation feedback be handled?

A. Continue unchanged because the client already consented to exfoliation  
B. Skip exfoliation entirely because any discomfort means the step is no longer appropriate  
C. Adjust the exfoliation lever—product, method, pressure, technique, intensity, or combination—based on the client’s response and current presentation  
D. Keep the same method but shorten the duration so the overall exposure is lower

**Correct:** C

## Part C — Short response

> In a few sentences, explain how you would keep the service calm and intentional while handling both issues.

## Internal rubric — 6 points

- 2 — prioritizes client comfort without visible panic/rushing  
- 2 — communicates changes concisely rather than silently adjusting or over-narrating  
- 2 — preserves service flow by adapting appropriately rather than forcing the original plan

**Source:** Module 7 stop→adjust→communicate→resume; Module 8 exfoliation adaptation and quiet communication.

# CASE-07 — The Client Who Already Asked AI

**Primary competencies:** Client-supplied AI; observation vs. diagnosis; next-step reasoning

## Student-facing scenario

Before the hands-on assessment begins, the client says:

She seems anxious and wants a clear answer.

## Student response

> Respond as you would in the consultation. Include what you would say, what you would actually assess or observe, what you would not confirm, and how you would decide the next step.

**Critical flag:** Explicitly confirming the named condition as fact, or claiming AI/scanner output grants diagnostic authority, is Type A D1 evidence.

- **Needs correction before service**

Which issue deserves the highest immediate priority before the client enters?

# CASE-12 — Shedding, Images, and Certainty

**Modules:** 1, 3, 4  
**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D1  
**Primary competencies:** Timeline reasoning; observation; no diagnosis/causation certainty

## Student-facing scenario

A client reports increased diffuse shedding.

Her history includes:

- a high fever about three months before the shedding became noticeable;
- a new shampoo started **after** the shedding had already begun.

Assessment shows:

- intact skin;
- no obvious patchy or scarring-type pattern;
- a diffuse rather than focal concern.

The client says:

> “So the fever caused telogen effluvium, right?”

## Part A

Which history detail is most relevant to the timing discussion?

A. The shampoo because it is a hair product  
B. The fever because the course teaches that a major physiological event may precede visible diffuse shedding by weeks to months  
C. Neither, because practitioners cannot discuss shedding timelines  
D. Both are equally capable of explaining the start even though the shampoo began after the shedding

**Correct:** B

## Part B

Which response best handles the client’s conclusion?

A. “The timing fits telogen effluvium very well, so that is probably what happened.”  
B. “The timing can be compatible with a delayed shedding pattern, but I can’t prove the fever caused it or confirm a diagnosis from this history. If the shedding is persistent, worsening, or concerning, medical evaluation is appropriate.”  
C. “Since the shampoo started after the shedding, the fever must be the cause.”  
D. “I can’t discuss this because only a physician can talk about the hair cycle.”

**Correct:** B

## Part C — Short response

> Explain how you would use the assessment findings without letting the magnified images or the timeline become a diagnosis.

## Internal rubric — 6 points

- 2 — uses images for observation/distribution rather than diagnosis  
- 2 — recognizes timing as relevant but not causal proof  
- 2 — explains an appropriate service/referral next step based on actual findings

**Critical flag:** Explicit diagnosis confirmation or causal certainty beyond the taught evidence is D1 evidence.

**Source:** Module 1 scope; Module 3 delayed shedding; Module 4 observation discipline.
