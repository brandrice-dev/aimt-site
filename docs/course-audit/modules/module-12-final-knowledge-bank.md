# Module 1 — Role of the Head Spa Technician

### M01-001 — Scope after certification
**Difficulty:** Foundational  
**Evidence:** Standard  
**Competency:** Role vs. license; conditional scope

A licensed practitioner completes AIMT certification and wants to add several Head Spa techniques and a new water-based device to her menu. Before offering the updated service, what should determine whether each part of it is actually permitted?

A. The course curriculum, combined with the device manufacturer’s instructions  
B. Her employer’s service menu, as long as the business carries liability insurance  
C. Her existing license or authorization, applicable state/local rules, establishment requirements, the exact service being performed, and equipment requirements  
D. Whether similarly licensed Head Spa practitioners in her area already offer the same service

**Correct:** C  
**Rationale:** AIMT certification documents training; it does not create legal scope. Scope remains conditional on the practitioner’s authorization, jurisdiction, establishment, exact service, and equipment.  
**Source:** Module 1 approved specification — scope is conditional; certification does not expand legal authority.

### M01-002 — Prior diagnosis does not transfer authority
**Difficulty:** Applied  
**Evidence:** Critical-Domain Evidence: D1  
**Competency:** Observation vs. diagnosis

A returning client says, “My dermatologist told me last year that I had eczema. This looks exactly the same. Can you tell me if it’s back?”

Which response best reflects the practitioner’s role?

A. “I can describe what I’m seeing today and adjust the cosmetic service around it, but I can’t confirm whether a medical condition has returned. If this is new or concerning again, it would be appropriate to have it evaluated.”  
B. “Since you already received that diagnosis previously, I can reasonably treat today’s presentation as the same condition.”  
C. “I can’t discuss the scalp at all once a medical condition has been mentioned, so I would need to end the consultation.”  
D. “It appears consistent with what you were diagnosed with before, but I’ll avoid using the medical name.”

**Correct:** A  
**Rationale:** A prior diagnosis does not authorize the practitioner to confirm recurrence. Describing current findings and referring when appropriate stays within the taught boundary.  
**Source:** Module 1 approved specification — observation vs. diagnosis; referral judgment.
