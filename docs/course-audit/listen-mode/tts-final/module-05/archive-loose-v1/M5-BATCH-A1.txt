You'll turn evidence into a real service plan, learn what you can realistically change in a single appointment, set priority order when more than one concern shows up, adapt one protocol to more than one client in real time, and recognize the errors that make a protocol less intelligent, not more.

Section 5.1 — From evidence to action. The scalp doesn't need a label. The service needs a reason.

A useful assessment changes what you do next — how thoroughly you cleanse, whether you exfoliate, how much heat you use, where you place product, how much pressure you apply, or whether you pause entirely. The weak approach notices one feature, assigns one type, and runs the same protocol everywhere. The stronger approach combines what's visible with what the client reports, what happened recently, and how each region responds. Two clients with different presentations don't need the same service — and neither can be reduced to one word.

Ask four questions before you choose a step: what's visibly present? What does the client report? What's the safest service limit? And what should change in this region? [firmly] Limit first. Priority second. Region by region.

Section 5.2 — What you can actually change. Most protocol changes come from five levers, and skilled customization is usually a deliberate change in intensity, placement, duration, or omission — not a completely different service.

Cleansing: how thorough, whether one region needs more attention, whether repeating a cleanse is appropriate. More lather isn't the goal — a clean, comfortable finish is.

Exfoliation: whether it belongs at all, and if so, region, method, intensity, and contact time. Visible scale is not automatic permission to exfoliate.

Water and steam: temperature, duration, distance. Heat helps some clients and bothers others — steam is optional, not proof the service is more advanced.

Pressure and tempo: adjusted to comfort and region. Pressure is never a corrective tool — firm massage should never overpower tenderness, burning, or a reactive presentation.

And product placement and finish: where product belongs, how much, and how the finish should feel. Whole-scalp application isn't automatically more thorough.

[slowly] Product comes after the decision. Don't choose the protocol because you want to use a particular product — choose the service direction first, then select a compatible product within your license, training, and manufacturer instructions.
