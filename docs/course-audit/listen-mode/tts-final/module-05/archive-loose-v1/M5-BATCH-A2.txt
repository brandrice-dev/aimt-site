Section 5.3 — Priority order. The safest limit outranks the client's request. Clients may ask for the deepest cleanse or the firmest pressure — their preference matters, but it doesn't override what the assessment tells you is appropriate today.

One, safety limit: is there any reason to avoid the area, pause, or refer? Broken, bleeding, weeping, oozing, draining, pustular, severely painful, or rapidly worsening findings don't need a stronger cosmetic protocol.

Two, client comfort and reactivity: stinging, burning, tenderness, itching, heat, tightness, recent chemical services, scratching, friction, or a recent product reaction.

Three, surface tolerance: whether cleansing, exfoliation, heat, or friction is likely to make the current presentation less comfortable.

Four, visible cosmetic need: what oil, residue, loose scale, or regional difference can be addressed conservatively.

And five, client preference and maintenance goal — once the limits are clear, shape the experience around comfort and what the client actually wants.

[warmly] The strongest service isn't the one with the most steps. It's the one where every step has a reason, and nothing gets added just to prove value.

Section 5.4 — Patterns, not permanent types. Read the dominant need without forcing the whole scalp into it.

Baseline or maintenance: no dominant concern, no meaningful regional difference, no discomfort — after checking wash timing and any recent chemical service, preserve. Gentle, non-disruptive cleanse, no unnecessary exfoliation, focus on comfort. Don't conclude the scalp has one universal "healthy" color, or that every corrective step should be added just because the client paid for a premium service.

Oil-dominant or residue-present: diffuse shine, slick roots, visible coating — after checking wash timing, dry shampoo, and whether it changes after cleansing, cleanse thoroughly but proportionately, with targeted placement and full rinse. Don't conclude the follicles are clogged or that cleansing will trigger compensatory oil production.

Fine-scale or dry-appearing: matte surface, fine loose scale, reported tightness — after checking itching, burning, and cleansing frequency, lower unnecessary cleansing intensity, use comfortable water temperature, reduce friction. Don't conclude the image proves dehydration, barrier damage, or dermatitis.

Mixed regional: shine in one region, scale or sensitivity in another — map the regions, and cleanse, exfoliate, condition, or reduce stimulation only where each decision is earned. Don't let one dominant feature control the protocol across the entire scalp.

And reactive presentation or reported sensitivity: visible difference from baseline plus stinging, burning, tenderness, or a recent reaction — reduce stimulation, lower heat, avoid aggressive exfoliation and firm massage, pause or refer when the presentation falls outside cosmetic limits. Don't conclude visible color alone proves sensitivity, inflammation, or infection.
