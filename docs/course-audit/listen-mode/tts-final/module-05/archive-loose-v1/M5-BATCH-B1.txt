Section 5.5 — One appointment, more than one approach. The protocol can change without the service feeling fragmented. Use the same structure, then adjust the levers by area — cleanse the crown more thoroughly, skip exfoliation near a reactive hairline, lower pressure at the temples, use a lighter finish at the roots. The client still experiences one cohesive service. You're just no longer pretending every square inch has the same need.

Five categories: preserve — keep what's already appropriate, don't remove a comfortable, effective part of the service just because another area needs modification. Modify — change intensity, placement, duration, temperature, pressure, or product amount. Avoid — omit a step that's unnecessary, incompatible, or likely to increase discomfort. Pause — stop when new symptoms, visible changes, contamination, or discomfort make continuing inappropriate. And refer — recommend medical evaluation when the presentation is outside cosmetic limits.

[slowly] Document the regional decision in plain language: what you observed, what the client reported, what you changed. Never document a diagnosis you're not qualified to make.

Section 5.6 — Choose a function, not a fantasy. Product knowledge matters, but product enthusiasm can distort judgment. A practitioner who starts with the bottle often finds a reason to use it — start with the service need instead.

Gentle routine cleansing, for maintenance and comfort. Targeted or more thorough cleansing, when visible oil or residue and client history support it. Optional compatible exfoliation, only when the skin is intact, the client is comfortable, and the product directions support it. Conditioning or hydration support, for comfort and softness — never described as treating a medical barrier disorder. And a minimal-product service, when the client reports recent reactivity or product history is unclear.

[firmly] Specific ingredients, medicated claims, and condition-directed products may carry license, labeling, contraindication, or manufacturer requirements. Follow the product — not a social-media protocol.

Section 5.7 — Intensity is part of the protocol. More heat, more pressure, and more time don't equal more expertise.

Steam may support comfort, but it's optional — reduce or omit it for heat sensitivity or a recent reaction, and never claim it opens pores or detoxifies. Water temperature: comfortably lukewarm as a default, cooler when heat increases discomfort. Pressure: controlled and responsive to feedback, never a corrective response to oil, scale, or residue. And time: longer contact isn't automatically better — use the shortest effective step that supports the goal.

[slowly] The protocol isn't the number of steps. It's the quality of the decisions between them.
