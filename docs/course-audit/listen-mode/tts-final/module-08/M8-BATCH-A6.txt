Chapter six — Neck and Shoulder Massage. How to do it: transition to neck, upper traps, and shoulder work — meaningfully longer in the Extended reference than in Core.

[seriously] Neck, shoulder, hand, and forearm work is offered only within your applicable professional scope, your training, and this client's consent for this specific service. Completing this course does not expand what you are licensed to perform — confirm your own state and salon requirements before including this work in a service.

Why we do it: the Head Spa experience does not have to stop at the hairline. Neck and shoulder work extends the hands-on relaxation experience beyond the scalp and helps maintain continuity between service phases — it should feel like part of one complete service, not a disconnected massage inserted in the middle.

How to communicate it. Communication cue, transition: "I'm going to move into your neck and shoulders now. Let me know if you'd like more or less pressure at any point." If they ask why this is part of the service: "This extends the relaxation experience beyond the scalp and helps keep the service feeling continuous rather than stopping the hands-on flow at the hairline."

Watch for in the demonstration: how the transition into the neck and shoulders is paced — this combined scalp, neck, and shoulder massage block runs about ten minutes in Core and about twenty minutes in Extended.

Chapter seven — Shampoo and Rinse. How to do it: second rinse, same method, no over-explanation — the client is deeply relaxed. Shampoo applied with therapeutic massage technique, not standard lather. Final rinse at consistent temperature.

Why we do it: this is the cleansing phase of the Head Spa protocol, not simply a standard salon shampoo dropped into the service. Controlled pressure, scalp focus, rhythm, and water-temperature awareness carry forward here while appropriately clearing the prior product and treatment.

How to communicate it — keep the flow quiet. This should generally remain quiet. The client is already immersed in the experience and there is usually no reason to narrate shampooing or rinsing. If they ask: "I'm using this product here because it fits what we're focusing on for your scalp and hair today."

[slowly] Watch for in the demonstration: the shift in energy here — the client is deeply relaxed, so this sequence runs with minimal narration.
