Section 8.2 — The service map. The masterclass, one chapter at a time.

Use each chapter to study more than the technique itself. Pay attention to why the step appears where it does, what to watch for, where judgment matters, and how to adapt the service without breaking the flow.

Before we start the chapters, one principle worth understanding clearly: explain intentionally, not continuously.

This service is relaxation-first — most of it stays quiet, not narrated step by step. The scripts you'll hear in each chapter aren't lines to recite during service; they're prepared answers. Learn them so that if a client asks a question, you can answer in a sentence or two, confidently, and return attention to the treatment — not think on the spot or ramble. Each chapter labels its communication guidance one of three ways: "Communication cue," meaning say this proactively; "Keep the flow quiet," meaning no narration needed by default; or "If they ask," meaning your ready answer if a question comes up.

And one more thing before the chapters begin: the service protocol itself is explained to the client at intake — that's where adaptations, omissions, and preferences are identified. During treatment, the client shouldn't have to repeatedly decide whether each standard step happens. Communication manages comfort, pressure, temperature, positioning, and real transitions instead.

[slowly] One more system to know before we start: each chapter carries small timing pills — pacing landmarks, not rules — showing roughly how much of the selected Core or Extended reference protocol remains at that point in the service. Steps one and two are the pre-timer opening; the treatment clock itself starts at Step three. These aren't rigid deadlines, absolute timing rules, or pass-fail stop times — client needs, product instructions, technique requirements, and your own judgment can all shift the actual pace. The masterclass teaches the technique. These markers teach the rhythm. The A I M T Service Timer, later in this module, turns that rhythm into a live protocol companion you can run during service.
