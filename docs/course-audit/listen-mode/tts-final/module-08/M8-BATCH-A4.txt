Chapter two — Client Positioning and Comfort. How to do it: help the client get fully settled and supported on the treatment bed — head, neck, and shoulders comfortably aligned — before the service continues. Confirm comfort directly rather than assuming it; this is the moment to adjust support, draping, or positioning before momentum makes that harder to do smoothly.

Why we do it: positioning creates the physical foundation for everything that follows. A fully supported client can remain settled instead of repeatedly adjusting the head, neck, shoulders, or body — protecting service continuity and letting you work without repeatedly interrupting the treatment to reposition the client.

How to communicate it. Communication cue: "Let's get you comfortable first. Tell me if anything needs adjusting before we start." If they ask why positioning matters: "I want your head and neck fully supported so you can relax without having to hold any tension or reposition yourself during the service."

Watch for in the demonstration: how the client is settled into position — physical comfort, support, and consent checked before anything else begins.

Chapter three — Dry Brushing and Hair Play. How to do it: work the brush across the scalp and through the lengths, distributing natural oils, ahead of the wet portion of the service.

Why we do it: this is the tactile bridge between the pre-timer opening and the wet service. It establishes tempo, introduces sustained hands-on contact, and gives the service a deliberate beginning before water enters the experience.

How to communicate it — keep the flow quiet. This does not normally need narration. Let the tactile, sensory experience stand on its own. If they ask: "I'm dry brushing through the scalp and hair before we move into the wet portion of the service."

[slowly] Watch for in the demonstration: brush-to-scalp contact and hand transitions — a short step that sets the tempo for what follows. This is where the treatment clock itself starts.

Chapter four — Halo Activation and Wet Massage. How to do it: activate the halo. Begin scalp massage under water flow. Check temperature with your hand before any client contact — even when the thermostat is set.

Why we do it: the Halo helps the wet phase feel integrated. Water flow, temperature, placement, touch, and massage operate as one continuous transition rather than rinse, stop, massage — the goal is continuity and a controlled sensory experience.

How to communicate it. Communication cue: "How does the water temperature feel? Let me know anytime if you want it warmer or cooler." Keep the flow quiet: once temperature is established, the wet massage should generally return to quiet. If they ask: "This is the halo rinse. It gives me a steady flow of water while I work through the scalp."

One note: temperature check is non-negotiable. Equipment fails. Your hand is the safeguard.

[slowly] Watch for in the demonstration: the temperature check before first water contact — every time, regardless of what the thermostat says.
