Chapter eight — Deep Conditioning and Hand-and-Arm Massage. How to do it, in Core: conditioning treatment, brief processing, rinse. In Extended: masque, steam, and processing, rinse.

Follow the product and equipment directions for processing time and temperature. Steam adds warmth and client comfort during processing — treatment selection and timing follow what the product calls for and how this client's scalp is responding today, not a fixed penetration claim.

Work hands and forearms while treatment processes. Use massage lotion. Cover both hands fully, fingertips to elbow — implement it whenever it's within your scope, you're trained, and nothing from intake indicates it should be skipped or adapted.

[seriously] Neck, shoulder, hand, and forearm work is offered only within your applicable professional scope, your training, and this client's consent for this specific service. Completing this course does not expand what you are licensed to perform — confirm your own state and salon requirements before including this work in a service.

Why we do it: the conditioning and treatment portion supports the actual hair and scalp service plan and follows the product's legitimate processing requirements. Processing time should still feel intentional — hand and forearm massage makes productive use of otherwise passive processing time and keeps the service experience active and connected, wherever it fits your scope, training, and service design.

How to communicate it. Communication cue, transition: "While your treatment processes, I'm going to move into the hand and forearm massage. Let me know if you'd like more or less pressure at any point." If they ask why it is included: "We use the processing time intentionally so the service stays active and connected instead of leaving that portion of the appointment inactive."

One note: explain the format difference clearly during booking — clients who've had the Extended service will notice the absence of steam and hand massage in Core.

[slowly] Watch for in the demonstration: the difference in depth between formats during conditioning, and — in the Extended reference — full hand-and-forearm coverage while the treatment processes.
