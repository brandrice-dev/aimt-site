Section 7.1 — The treatment bed. Your most important equipment decision.

The bed is the foundation of everything — comfort, positioning, water flow, massage access, client perception, all of it builds from here. A halo-equipped wet bed is the foundation of a true head spa service; the halo allows water immersion during massage in a way dry work can't replicate.

[firmly] What most people get wrong: they choose based on how it looks, not how it performs. A bed with restrictive armrests may photograph beautifully, but larger clients feel confined, taller clients feel compressed, movement becomes limited — and once comfort breaks, the entire service is affected.

Evaluate any bed against what it needs to do, not how it looks, across seven categories. Basin and head support — the headrest curve should actually support the occipital bone in a relaxed position. Client entry, exit, and stability — safe on and off, no shift or wobble. Practitioner reach and working height — affects your posture across an entire shift, not just one service. Water management — how it's contained and drained affects flow and sanitation. Cleaning and sanitation compatibility — surfaces you can actually disinfect, not just wipe. And two preference categories, not universal requirements: space requirements, which depend on your specific business, and armrest configuration, a commonly reported comfort preference among practitioners, not a documented requirement.

[slowly] There's no single required bed model. Evaluating any bed against these categories — instead of how it photographs — is what makes the decision transferable to whatever you're actually offered or can afford. A common early mistake is prioritizing what looks impressive in photos over what actually feels comfortable to lie in for an hour. Choose the bed your clients will want to come back to.
