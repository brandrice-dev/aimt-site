Section 7.2 — Tools and supplies. Essentials first. Upgrades later.

Most people overvalue tools — they assume more tools means a better service. Your hands create the experience. Tools support, enhance, and assist, but they should never replace presence. Overuse leads to a mechanical-feeling service and inconsistent pressure. Clients may not say it, but they feel it.

On screen, four expandable categories separate what's essential from what's an upgrade: linens and comfort, service tools, sanitation supplies, and the ambient experience. Start with everything on the essentials list — nothing on the upgrade list is necessary to deliver a premium service. Add from the upgrade tier once your volume justifies it.

Where something lives on your cart matters as much as whether you own it. Arrange by how often you reach for it during a service, not by those four buying categories. The within-reach zone, shoulder-to-waist height, no step required: your product dishes and applicator brush — everything you touch during nearly every step. The one-step zone: items used once or twice per service, like your comb or blow dryer. And the reserve zone, off the working surface entirely: backup linens, upgrade tools not in use, sanitation stock — keeping these off the working surface keeps your clean-and-dirty separation clean and visible during the live service, not just at setup.

Section 7.3 — Station prep sequence. Run this before every single client.

Inconsistency in setup is invisible to you and obvious to your client — they notice when the towel warmer is cold or the cart's in the wrong place, even if they can't say why the experience felt off. The order isn't arbitrary — it follows a build sequence: sanitation and structural setup first, then product and tool staging, then comfort, then ambiance, so nothing gets assembled while the room's being sanitized or the client's already in it.

Halo flush — Barbicide rinse, then plain water until clear. Product dishes — three bowls, shampoo, mask, exfoliant, applicator brush alongside. Towel warmer loaded — confirm warm to the touch before the client arrives. Fresh bed linens. Bed warmer on, set appropriately. Room temperature set lower than normal, since the client will be wet. Spa wrap, robe, and slipper socks folded and ready at the station entry. A towel wrapped around the halo nozzle hose — quiets it, prevents spray, maintains atmosphere. Massage oil and lotion ready on the cart. And ambient — music, lighting, diffuser — running before the client enters the room.
