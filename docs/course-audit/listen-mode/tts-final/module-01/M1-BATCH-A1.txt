Welcome to Module 1 — Role, Scope, and Professional Boundaries. Know what you are. Know what you are not.

Professionalism begins before the service does — with how you observe, explain, adapt, and stay within scope.

In this module, you'll: define the head spa technician's role, and its limits; separate professional observation from medical diagnosis; understand what depends on your license and local scope; set honest expectations for what a head spa can support; and recognize when referral is the right professional decision.

[warmly] Pay attention to this: the language you use when describing what you see. That distinction follows you through the rest of the course.

Section 1.1 — What is a head spa? It is not just a shampoo. It is not just a massage. And it is not a medical treatment.

A head spa is a structured, scalp-focused service designed to support scalp hygiene, comfort, and the overall scalp environment — while delivering a deeply relaxing, sensory experience. Those two things coexist. The relaxation is real. So is the structure underneath it.

On screen, six elements make up that structure. Cleansing — the foundation of every service. Exfoliation — when appropriate, not automatically. Massage and relaxation — the sensory core of the service. Water therapy — temperature, immersion, sensory shift. Conditioning and treatment — product choice driven by what you observe. And sensory elements — temperature, touch, sound, aromatherapy.

Here's something I'd add. From the client's perspective, it feels like relaxation. From your perspective, it should feel controlled and intentional. That's the difference. Anyone can make something feel relaxing for a moment. Very few people can deliver a consistent, high-quality experience that holds together from beginning to end.

Section 1.2 — What is a head spa technician? You are responsible for the full experience. Not just the steps.

This is where most people underestimate the role. They think: "I just need the steps." That works until something doesn't look the way you expected.

In this course, "head spa technician" describes the role you perform during a head spa service. It is not a separate state license. Your legal scope comes from the license or authorization you already hold, the laws where you practice, and the specific services you perform. A I M T certification documents course completion; it does not expand that legal scope.

The work behind the calm: the service should feel effortless to the client. Behind that calm, the practitioner is observing, communicating, adjusting pressure and pacing, protecting scope, recognizing when to simplify or stop, and maintaining control from beginning to end.
