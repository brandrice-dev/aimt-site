Section 4.3 — Image integrity. A useful image begins before the lens touches the scalp.

Magnification can exaggerate a poor setup. Standardize the conditions before you interpret the screen. Six steps, in order.

One — start before treatment: assess before water, exfoliants, oils, masks, steam, or treatment products alter the surface.

Two — ask what is already on the scalp: record wash timing, dry shampoo, root sprays, oils, fibers, medicated products, chemical services, and recent scratching or irritation.

Three — part cleanly: create a clear view without scraping or repeatedly disturbing the same area.

Four — touch lightly: rest the device steadily. Excess pressure can blanch or redden the skin and create the finding you think you discovered.

Five — hold the view: stay long enough to inspect the image. A constant visual sweep produces motion, blur, and shallow interpretation.

Six — make comparisons honest: match region, magnification, light, pressure, orientation, and device settings when comparing two images.

[firmly] A sanitation note: clean and disinfect reusable contact surfaces before and after each client according to the manufacturer's instructions and applicable rules. Do not place the device on broken, bleeding, weeping, oozing, or draining skin.

If the setup changes, the image changes. Document the difference before you call it improvement.

Section 4.4 — The five-point scalp scan. Five assessment points prevent one-angle thinking.

The scalp is not uniform. A I M T's baseline scan uses the same five assessment points in the same order so the practitioner does not let one dramatic image represent the entire head.

Memory line: front, top, crown, side, back.

Point one of five — the frontal hairline. Purpose: establish the visible condition along the hairline, where styling products, cosmetics, tension, scale, and client concerns may be easiest to notice. Technique cue: separate hair gently and avoid rubbing the hairline before the image is captured.

Point two of five — the top parting. Purpose: collect a consistent central view of the scalp surface, follicular openings, and hair distribution along the part. Technique cue: use the same part location when creating a future comparison.

Point three of five — the crown, or vertex. Purpose: compare the crown with the parting and hairline rather than assuming the central scalp presents uniformly. Technique cue: work with the natural swirl instead of forcing the shafts flat across the view.

Point four of five — the temporal area. Purpose: observe a lateral region and compare it with the central scalp. Technique cue: check the opposite side when asymmetry, tension, tenderness, or localized concern is reported.

Point five of five — the occipital area, at the back. Purpose: include the hidden posterior scalp, which may look different from the areas the client sees and styles most often. Technique cue: position the hair securely so the lens remains steady and the client does not have to hold an uncomfortable posture.

A five-point scan creates the baseline. The client's concern tells you where to add targeted views.
