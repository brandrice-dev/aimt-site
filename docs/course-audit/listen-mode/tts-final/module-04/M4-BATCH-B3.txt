Section 4.9 — Practitioner insight. The device can create the evidence you think you found.

Pressure changes color: excess contact can blanch or redden the scalp. Capture the baseline before repeated contact alters it.

A dirty lens creates a false story: haze, residue, oil, or scale on the device can appear to belong to the client. Inspect and prepare the lens before the scan.

Products can imitate pathology: dry shampoo, fibers, root spray, oils, masks, and medicated products can change surface appearance. Ask before you interpret.

One image is not the scalp: a dramatic crown image does not erase a calm hairline, and a clean hairline does not represent the hidden posterior scalp.

Before-and-after requires matching conditions: different light, angle, pressure, magnification, or location can create a more dramatic "result" than the service did.

Quick reality check: microscopy does not become advanced when the image is dramatic. It becomes advanced when the collection method is consistent and the interpretation is restrained.

Section 4.10 — Common mistakes. What to unlearn before the camera makes it look convincing. Six patterns.

Naming the scalp after one image. The fix: complete the five-point scan and compare distribution before forming a working service direction.

Pressing for a clearer view. The fix: improve parting, stability, focus, lighting, and lens cleanliness — not pressure.

Turning visible material into a cause. The fix: describe location, color, texture, and distribution — ask about product and wash history.

Saving images without clear permission. The fix: separate consent for live view, record capture, teaching, marketing, and external sharing.

Creating a misleading before-and-after. The fix: match region, magnification, light, pressure, and orientation.

Performing assessment that changes nothing. The fix: the image should support preserve, modify, avoid, pause, or refer. Otherwise it is theater.

[firmly] The microscope is not the skill. The collection method, language, and decision are the skill.
