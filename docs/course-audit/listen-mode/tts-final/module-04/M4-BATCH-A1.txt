[warmly] Welcome to Module Four — Microscopy and Scalp Assessment. Stop assuming. Start seeing.

Magnification does not hand you a diagnosis. It gives you a better view. This module teaches a repeatable five-point scan, disciplined observation, and the moment a cosmetic service should be customized, simplified, paused, or referred.

In this module, you'll: learn a repeatable five-point scalp scan you can use every time; present magnified findings to a client without overstating what you see; apply the five observation lenses to real, varied scalp images; know the moment a service should be customized, simplified, or paused; and see the common mistakes new practitioners make when reading images.

[warmly] Pay attention to this: two scalps can look nearly identical and still call for two different decisions — the image is evidence, not the whole story.

Section 4.1 — The role of magnification. A closer view is not a final answer.

A scalp camera can reveal details that are easy to miss with the unaided eye: surface shine, scale, residue, material around follicular openings, hair-shaft variation, and differences from one region to another.

That does not mean the device knows what caused what you see. Medical trichoscopy is a clinical diagnostic technique interpreted alongside history, examination, and other medical information. In a head spa, scalp microscopy is a cosmetic observation and education tool.

Its value is precision, not certainty. It helps you collect better evidence before you choose a cosmetic service direction.

The image can sharpen the question. It cannot answer every question.

From Cadence: "A weak assessment names the scalp too quickly. A strong assessment collects the view, compares regions, asks for context, and lets the service decision remain no more certain than the evidence."

Section 4.2 — Presenting the assessment. Make the lens feel informative, not invasive.

Tell the client what the camera is for before it touches the scalp. The assessment should feel like part of customization, not a search for something wrong.

Here's the live-view script, exactly as written: "Before we begin, I'm going to look at several areas of your scalp under magnification. I'll use what is visible — along with what you tell me — to customize the cosmetic service. The camera helps us see detail, but it does not diagnose a scalp condition."

And the image-capture consent script: "I can use the camera for a live view without saving anything. I can also capture comparison images for your service record. Would you like me to save them, and may I explain how they will be stored and used?"

A privacy note: permission to view is not permission to save. Permission to save for the client record is not permission to post, teach from, transmit to another service, or use in marketing.

Here's the client-language rule: show the image without making the client feel dirty, damaged, or alarmed. Describe the finding. Explain the cosmetic decision. State the limit when the cause cannot be determined.
