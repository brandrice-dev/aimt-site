Section 4.3 — Image integrity. A useful image begins before the lens touches the scalp. Magnification can exaggerate a poor setup — standardize the conditions before you interpret the screen.

One: start before treatment — assess before water, exfoliants, oils, masks, steam, or treatment products alter the surface. Two: ask what's already on the scalp — wash timing, dry shampoo, root sprays, oils, fibers, medicated products, chemical services, recent scratching or irritation. Three: part cleanly, without scraping or repeatedly disturbing the same area. Four: touch lightly — excess pressure can blanch or redden the skin and create the finding you think you discovered. Five: hold the view long enough to actually inspect it; a constant sweep produces motion, blur, and shallow interpretation. And six: make comparisons honest — match region, magnification, light, pressure, orientation, and device settings when comparing two images.

[firmly] Clean and disinfect reusable contact surfaces before and after each client, per the manufacturer's instructions and applicable rules. Never place the device on broken, bleeding, weeping, oozing, or draining skin. If the setup changes, the image changes — document the difference before you call it improvement.
