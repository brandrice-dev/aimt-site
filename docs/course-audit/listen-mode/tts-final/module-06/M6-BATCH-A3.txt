Section 6.4 — The cycle worth understanding. Why the wrong product makes things worse.

This is a common, avoidable pattern: a client sees flakes, assumes dandruff, and reaches for the strongest anti-dandruff product they can find. Here's what actually happens, in order — there's an interactive walk-through on screen if you want to open each stage yourself.

One: the client notices flaking on their clothing and scalp. This is where it starts. A client sees flakes and immediately interprets them as dandruff. The assumption is almost universal — and almost always made without any real assessment.

Two: they assume it must be dandruff and buy anti-dandruff shampoo. The product choice follows the assumption. Anti-dandruff shampoos are heavily marketed, widely available, and seem like the logical solution. The mistake is reaching for the wrong one.

Three: the anti-dandruff shampoo strips the scalp of its remaining moisture and oils. Anti-dandruff shampoos strip oil, suppress yeast, and slow cell turnover. On a scalp that already lacks moisture, they remove what little barrier is left.

Four: the scalp becomes drier, flaking worsens, and there's more irritation. The scalp responds to barrier loss by becoming drier, tighter, and more reactive. Flaking increases. The client interprets this as the condition getting worse.

Five: the client increases usage, thinking they need more of the same product. The logical response to a product not working is to use more of it. This accelerates the damage cycle.

Six: back to step one. The cycle continues, and they arrive at your table having over-treated for months. By the time they reach you, the scalp has been compromised repeatedly. Your first job is often to stop the damage, not start a new treatment.

[warmly] Here's a note from me: this is not treatment failure. It's misidentification. Flaking alone does not justify anti-dandruff treatment. Your job is to identify what type of flaking you're seeing before recommending anything. Sometimes the best thing you can do is simplify — not add more.
