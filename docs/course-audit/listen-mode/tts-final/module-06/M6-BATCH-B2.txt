Section 6.6 — When to pause or refer. Know these without needing to touch anything.

Refer when you see: redness or scaling that spreads beyond the scalp — to the eyebrows, hairline, or ears — combined with active irritation. Broken, weeping, crusted, or bleeding skin. Symptoms the client describes as severe, painful, or rapidly worsening. No improvement despite reasonable home care and product changes. Or any presentation you are not confident matches a typical dry-scalp or dandruff-spectrum pattern — including anything that could resemble a different condition, the way we covered back in 6.2.

[slowly] Here's the referral script: "I'm seeing something today that goes beyond what a head spa service is designed to address. I don't want to guess at the cause or risk making it worse, so I'd recommend having it evaluated before we continue with scalp services."

Signature interaction: sort three presentations. For each presentation, decide whether to proceed as usual, proceed with a modification, or pause and refer. Weigh the combination of cues together — not any single feature in isolation. There are three options for each one: proceed as usual, proceed with modification, or pause and refer. I'll walk through all three for each presentation.

Presentation one: fine white powdery flakes, matte surface, minimal oil, mild tightness, no redness.
Proceed as usual — not quite: treating this as your standard service overlooks the barrier-supportive adjustments a dry-scalp presentation like this one calls for. Proceed with modification — correct: the matte, powdery, minimal-oil presentation supports a dry-scalp-pattern direction — a modified, hydration-supportive routine, with simplification if the client reports over-treating. Pause and refer — not quite: nothing described here — no redness, no spread, no severe discomfort — meets a referral trigger. This presentation can be served with adjustment, not paused.

Presentation two: yellowish, clumped flakes, visible oil at the root, mild redness, contained to the scalp, no spread.
Proceed as usual — not quite: visible oil and mild redness call for cleansing support and an OTC-category recommendation, not the unmodified standard service. Proceed with modification — correct: oil, clumped yellowish flakes, and redness contained to the scalp support a modified, cleansing-forward direction with an OTC-category anti-fungal or zinc recommendation, plus monitoring. Pause and refer — not quite: the redness here is mild and contained to the scalp, with no spread — that doesn't meet the threshold for pausing the service and referring out.

Presentation three: scale and redness spreading to the eyebrows and hairline, the client reports significant discomfort, and the pattern has persisted for months despite drugstore treatments.
Proceed as usual — not quite: spread beyond the scalp with active redness and significant discomfort are referral-relevant findings — this isn't a standard-service presentation. Proceed with modification — not quite: a modified service still assumes proceeding is appropriate. Spread to the eyebrows and hairline, combined with significant discomfort and months of unresolved symptoms, calls for stepping back from the service, not adjusting it. Pause and refer — correct: spread beyond the scalp with active redness, real discomfort, and months without improvement despite home treatment are referral-relevant findings — this is a pause-and-refer decision, not a service one.

[firmly] Across all three, the same read applies: weigh redness, spread, discomfort, and duration together, and let the combination — not any one feature — decide whether you proceed, modify, or refer.
