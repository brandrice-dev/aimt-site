Section 6.5 — Malassezia to seborrheic dermatitis: one spectrum. Not two conditions. One spectrum.

Malassezia is a naturally occurring yeast found on most scalps. It becomes a problem when several factors line up together — increased oil, a shift in the scalp's surface environment, and individual variation in how the scalp's immune and inflammatory response reacts to the yeast's activity. As it progresses, it moves along a spectrum — the same underlying process, at different levels of severity. There's an interactive slider on screen with four positions. Here's what each one looks like.

Balanced: a balanced scalp has Malassezia present at normal levels — no visible flaking, no redness, no irritation. Sebum production is balanced.

Mild dandruff: early Malassezia-driven dandruff shows small white to yellow flakes, slight clustering, mild oil, minimal redness. Still within scope with appropriate product choices.

Moderate: at the moderate stage, flakes are more visible, oil presence is notable, and there's mild redness. The client likely notices this daily. A head spa can support this — consistent home care is also needed.

Seborrheic dermatitis: this stage shows larger, thicker flakes, pronounced redness, and flaking that can spread beyond the scalp to the eyebrows, hairline, or ears. We'll cover exactly when to pause or refer next.
