Section 6.7 — Treatment within scope. You're supporting the environment. Not treating the condition.

Cleansing support: regular cleansing reduces excess oil, buildup, and the microbial overgrowth Malassezia feeds on. Consistency matters more than intensity.

Anti-fungal supporting ingredients: zinc pyrithione, 1%-strength ketoconazole, and 1% selenium sulfide are OTC categories that help manage yeast activity. Never recommend or name a prescription-strength, 2%, product.

Anti-inflammatory botanicals: licorice root and chamomile calm the scalp, reducing redness, irritation, and discomfort without disrupting the barrier — preferred over aggressive treatments in a head spa context.

And regular maintenance: irregular care leads to fluctuating symptoms. The most effective approach is consistent support over time, not aggressive intervention when things flare.

[warmly] A product category is not a diagnosis. Recommending one is a retail suggestion within a cosmetic service — never a diagnosis, never a prescription. If a presentation meets any of the referral criteria, refer instead of recommending a product.

Section 6.8 — What makes it worse. Understanding what aggravates these conditions helps you educate clients in a way that actually changes their behavior.

Stress and hormonal changes: associated with flare-ups, most likely through a combination of immune and inflammatory response and changes in oil production — not oil production alone. Seasonal or cyclical worsening is often a hormonal pattern. Worth asking about at intake, not a diagnosis.

Diet high in sugar, fried, or inflammatory foods: may contribute for some clients, though the evidence is largely individual. Worth asking about — not something to lecture the client on.

Excess oil production: creates a favorable environment for Malassezia. Warmer weather, humidity, and hormonal shifts are commonly associated with more oil between washes, though the relationship isn't precise enough to apply as a fixed rule.

And wrong product use: the most correctable trigger. Overly harsh or stripping products worsen both dryness and dandruff. Clients often arrive already over-treating — simplification is often more effective than adding more.
