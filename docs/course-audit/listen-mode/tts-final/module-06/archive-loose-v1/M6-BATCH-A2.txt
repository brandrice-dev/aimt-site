Section 6.3 — The most important distinction in scalp care. Dry scalp and dandruff are not the same thing. They can both present with flaking, but the cause — and the treatment approach — is completely different. Getting this wrong is one of the most common and consequential mistakes in scalp care. There's a side-by-side illustration on screen comparing the two patterns.

Dry scalp is a hydration issue: small, white, powdery flakes, a matte, non-oily surface, tightness or mild itch, minimal visible oil. It flakes because it lacks moisture — the hydrolipid film is depleted, a barrier issue, not a disease process. Causes include aging, seasonal changes, over-cleansing, harsh products, chemical processing. The direction: gentle exfoliation to lift dead cells, then hydration and barrier repair — restore what's missing, don't suppress a biological process.

Dandruff is a biological imbalance: larger, yellowish flakes, oily or clumped near the root, visible oil at the scalp, irritation or redness. It's generally associated with the scalp environment shifting out of balance — excess oil, Malassezia yeast activity, inflammation, and faster skin-cell turnover typically combining, though the exact balance varies by client. This is not a hydration problem — adding moisture to an oily, inflamed scalp can make it worse. The direction: cleansing support, oil control, anti-fungal-supporting ingredients, anti-inflammatory botanicals — rebalance the environment, don't repair a barrier.

[slowly] Some clients show features of both. When that happens, don't force a label — ask about recent products, wash frequency, how long the pattern's been present, and favor the gentler direction until the picture is clearer.
