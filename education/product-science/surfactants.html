Surfactants and Cleansers: A Practitioner Education Overview

Surfactants are the cleansing agents in shampoo. Their oil-attracting and water-attracting parts help sebum be removed during rinsing, and formulators commonly combine several detergents to achieve the intended result.

How surfactants help cleansing

Begin with what the cleanser is designed to do.

Lather is a sensory and formulation feature. Foaming agents help create suds, while detergents do the cleansing; foam volume is not a direct measure of how well a shampoo cleans.

Understanding the complete formula

Shampoo formulation combines surfactant choices with other ingredients to achieve the intended cleansing and hair feel.

Alkyl glucosides, including decyl glucoside, are another group used mainly as surfactants. Some also have conditioning or emulsion-stabilizing functions.

Reading ingredient safety conclusions

The CIR assessment of alkyl glucosides concluded that the ingredients were safe under the practices and concentrations assessed when formulated to be nonirritating. That qualification is part of the conclusion.

What scalp cleansing research can show

A pilot study on human scalp measured lower levels of natural moisturizing factor components and a fraction of outer-skin lipids after repeated washing with a standard shampoo. This gives researchers a way to investigate cleansing-related dryness.

For professionals, the useful habit is to ask what was tested, in which formulation, and under which conditions.

What this page can — and cannot — tell you

Scope: This page explains surfactant cleansing, formulation context and selected scalp washout research. It does not rank brands or ingredient classes, claim that a sulfate-free or other label guarantees suitability, prescribe a washing schedule or contact time, provide formulation concentration limits, or diagnose or treat scalp disease. Ingredient-assessment conclusions remain limited to their stated conditions.

The alkyl-glucoside assessment reflects the use practices and concentrations assessed at publication; it is not a current audit of every finished shampoo.

The scalp washout finding concerns the tested shampoo and repeated-washing conditions. It does not establish the same response to every surfactant formula.
